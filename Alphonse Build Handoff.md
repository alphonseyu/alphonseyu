**Governing principle for this fire: VISUAL WEIGHT SHOULD MATCH CONSEQUENCE.**

- **▶ ALSO: REORDER THE CHIPS P/O/H → H · O · P** (added 24 Jul). Not just the pun — **Herald is used daily** (it's the work log, the anchor habit) while Overture and Presto are occasional, so **the most-toggled instrument should sit first.** Function and joke agree, which is the good case. ⚠️ **Colours travel with the labels** (Presto green / Overture blue / Herald amber are tied to each instrument's token), so this isn't a pure label swap — eyeball it after the fire to confirm it still reads cleanly.

- **P/O/H → into the edit state.** Alphonse's finding, and it reframes what looked like a layout problem: the row is crowded (drag handle + name + 3 chips + 3 icons), **but the real issue is SEMANTIC.** Flipping a chip is *consequential* — it changes where a Movement appears across the whole suite — yet a one-tap-anytime chip gives it the same weight as a decoration. **The clutter is a symptom; the problem is a meaningful decision sitting at casual weight.**
- **🗑️ DELETE → into the edit state too, at the BOTTOM, away from everything else.** Same principle, higher stakes: delete is the most destructive action on the row and currently sits at *identical visual weight to rename*, one fat-finger from a Movement vanishing. ⚠️ Note it isn't unprotected today (there's already a two-step tap-trash-then-confirm), **but that's RECOVERY from a mistap, not PREVENTION.** Moving it off the row prevents the mistap entirely, which is strictly better.
- **Resulting row: drag handle · name · ✏️ pencil · ⇄ move.** Clean, and every remaining action is non-destructive.
- **The edit/detail state holds:** name field · P/O/H visibility · and delete at the bottom, visually separated.
- ⚠️ **This is NOT a return to the hover-reveal mistake.** Hiding controls behind a *hover* was undiscoverable (hover doesn't exist on mobile). Putting *considered decisions* behind a *considered tap* is different — the gesture is discoverable, and the weight is now intentional.
- Rejected alternative (Rép's): wrapping P/O/H onto a second line. Declutters, but keeps a consequential toggle at one-tap weight — solves the symptom, not the problem.

**▶ ON-DEVICE RESULT (25 Jul, after firing) — bug FIXED, reversal DECLINED. Fire 5 CLOSED.**

### ▶ 🎼 SCORE'S STATS = A STAVE. "A Score on Score." (direction found 24 Jul)
The stats half of Score had sat unoperationalised since the spec was written — Alphonse knew he wanted per-Suite rollups but never worked out **what they'd look like**. A throwaway symbolic mockup (`symphon-a-score-for-the-working-day.html`, made purely for fun) accidentally answered it: **render the rollup as a musical score.**

**Why this isn't just aesthetic — the form matches the data shape:**
- **Score is the only instrument that sees ACROSS all the others** — every Movement carries `visibleIn` for Herald / Overture / Presto. So Score already holds the one thing a stave needs: **multiple parts playing simultaneously over one timeline.**
- A bar chart flattens that into a single series. **A stave is literally designed for several instruments across the same span of time.** Herald time, Presto completions, Overture status = three parts, one timeline, per Suite.
- **It solves why "per-Suite stats" felt vague:** a table of numbers per Suite is boring and unreadable. *"Here's this Suite as a movement"* is legible at a glance — you see instantly which Suites were loud this quarter and which went **tacet**.
- **▶ The appraisal argument (this is the real payoff):** an appraisal doesn't want *"47 tasks completed."* It wants **shape** — this project ramped in May, went quiet, came back hard in July. A stave shows that instantly. **It's the difference between a spreadsheet and a story, and appraisals are stories.**

**Elements the doodle got right and worth carrying over:**
- **Notes coloured by instrument**, positioned by time, height = whatever dimension is chosen (effort/priority).
- **RESTS ARE DRAWN, not omitted** — a quiet period is marked as part of the piece, not as absence. *(Same principle as Antiphon's Beep: a small flame is still a real entry.)*
- **`tacet`** for a Suite with no activity in the period — the correct musical term for an instrument that has a part and stays silent. Not missing, not broken: **deliberately not playing.**
- **Dynamics (`ff`/`mf`/`p`)** as an at-a-glance intensity marker per part.

**Name/pun (Alphonse, 24 Jul): "a Score on Score"** — and the second layer is accidentally the product description: **a score of your time literally is a record of the time of your life.**

Routing for 3.0 is a fresh question.

- **BUT one case genuinely earns it, and it's specific to this model: numbered entries are inherently addressable in a way dated ones never were.** "Motif No. 77" *wants* a handle. ### ▶ TITLES, TAGS, CHAPTERS — decided 23 Jul
- **✅ OPTIONAL TITLES — yes, never required.** Let one emerge *after* writing, or show the opening line in the catalogue so an entry names itself. Forcing a title before writing is just another blank to fill. **Why they earn their place:** "Motif No. 43" alone is a needle in a haystack once there are hundreds — a title is how you find something you half-remember.
- **⏸️ TAGS / CATEGORIES — much later, if ever.** ⚠️ The tension: this app exists to *remove* the obligation to classify. Tags reintroduce a decision at write-time ("which bucket?") — exactly the friction that made the dated model tiring. Supporting evidence from his own experience: he built Suites/Movements in Symphon and then found *by using it* that categorising is real work. **Revisit only if the lack genuinely bites.**
- **❌ CHAPTERS — NOT a feature (rejected 23 Jul, and the reasoning matters).** Initially floated as better than tags because chapters are *sequential* rather than classificatory. **But Alphonse spotted the flaw: grouping arbitrarily is still weird.** Someone must decide where one chapter ends and the next begins — that boundary is invented, not produced by the writing. **It's classification wearing a sequential costume.** Worse in the retroactive case: drawing chapter lines in hindsight imposes a narrative on thoughts that didn't have one — the recency-bias trap in a different shape.
  - **The only version that would work:** a boundary *given* rather than chosen — a real rupture (job change, move, loss) that he'd recognise without deciding. **But that can't be built in advance, only noticed afterwards.** If such a break ever happens, a simple manual marker ("this is where things changed") is trivial to add then. **Building the mechanism before the rupture is inventing structure for its own sake.**

**So the clean model: numbers as the only ordering · optional titles · no tags · no chapters.**

### ▶ DESIGN APPROACH: "CONTROLLED CHAOS" (decided 24 Jul)
Alphonse wants the agent to have creative latitude — but the latitude has a hard boundary, and the boundary is **which layer the chaos lands in.**

**🎲 FREEHAND — let the agent be inventive (cheap to change later):**
Layout, spacing, typography, motion, how the catalogue is arranged, what the detail/edit view feels like, empty states, transitions. These are surface: if something's odd, re-prompt and it changes. No data touched, no migration.

**🔒 LOCKED — do not let the agent reinterpret (expensive or impossible to change later):**
1. **THE DATA MODEL.** Numbered entries (never dates as the organising key), optional title, word-count-derived depth, a three-value effort field. ⚠️ **Change these after fifty Motifs exist and you're migrating your own writing** — the exact risk that made Emberling delicate and got the Score migration dropped.
2. **THE CIRCLE AND THE GLOW** (see above) — settled, twice-endorsed, the emotional centre of the app.
3. **The framing rules** — Unclassified is an inbox not a bin; a small flame is an invitation not a punishment; weight matches consequence.

**⚠️ THE SPECIFIC DANGER, and why the model must be locked hardest:** a freehand agent doesn't know this project — it knows "journal app." Its training says journals have **dates, streaks and calendar heatmaps**, which is *precisely* the thing Alphonse rediagnosed away from. Left loose, **it will rebuild Antiphon 2.0.** The model isn't an aesthetic preference — **the model IS the diagnosis.** If a date field creeps back in "for convenience," the original problem quietly returns.

**Net: chaos is safe ABOVE the data layer, dangerous below it.** Worst case under this split is *"I don't love this screen"* — one small fire to fix, never a migration.

**Mockups (`antiphon-notebook-concept.html`, `antiphon-reflection-mockup.html`) are REFERENCE, not gospel** — except the circle-and-glow, which is locked. If the agent finds something better within the constraints, take it.

**▶ ✅ NO FURTHER MOCKUPS NEEDED (decided 24 Jul).** The two that exist cover what actually needed *seeing*: the dark palette, the glow, the flame as a dual-channel mark, and the catalogue proving that big-flame-dull-colour reads as padding (the real test — it passed). **What's left unmocked is exactly the controlled-chaos territory** (layout, spacing, detail/edit view, transitions) — mocking it would defeat the point, handing the agent a picture to copy instead of constraints to work within.
- ⚠️ **A mockup is a CONTRACT:** the more that exist, the more the build becomes *"match these images"* rather than *"build this thing."* This spec is unusually strong on **behaviour and principle** (weight matches consequence · invitation not punishment · inbox not bin) — **principles travel better than pictures**, because an agent can apply a principle to a screen nobody mocked, but can't extrapolate from an image.
- **The edit/detail state is the one plausible exception** (brand new, holds P/O/H + delete, appears in neither mockup) — **still don't pre-mock it.** What goes in it and why is already specified; let the agent propose a layout and react to something real. Reacting is cheap; pre-specifying is expensive.

### ▶ PROMPT LANGUAGE — how to DESCRIBE the app to the agent (decided 24 Jul)
Word choice is the strongest lever over what the agent reaches for. It isn't cosmetic.

- **❌ NEVER say "journal app."** It summons the entire 2.0 furniture set: dates, calendar views, streaks, "How was your day?", mood pickers, heatmaps, one-entry-per-day. The model has seen ten thousand of these. **Saying "journal" is asking it to rebuild the thing that was rediagnosed away.**
- **✅ "Notebook" is much better** — it summons pages, sequence, free-form entries, no date obligation, something you flip through. Structurally close to the real design. **But it is a tailwind, not a guardrail:** plenty of notebook apps still date-stamp by default, so the explicit *"numbered, never dates as the organising key"* constraint must STILL be stated.
- ⚠️ **Watch what "notebook" drags in instead:** Notion-ish furniture — folders, nested pages, tags, rich-text toolbars, a sidebar tree. **Tags and chapters are already explicitly rejected**, so a folder structure sneaking back through the side door is a new failure mode replacing the old one.
- **▶ BEST: skip the category label entirely and describe the BEHAVIOUR.** Every category word carries baggage; a behavioural description has nothing to pattern-match against, so the agent must build what was said rather than what it has seen. Use something like:
  > *"A private writing app where entries are numbered, not dated — Motif No. 1, No. 2 — written when there's something to say rather than on a schedule."*
- If a label is wanted anyway, **"notebook" over "journal"**, always.

**▶ SIDE NOTE (24 Jul) — "Notion-ish" is RIGHT for the Conductor and POISON for Antiphon.** Worth recording because the same reference gives opposite verdicts for structural reasons, and it'll save a bad call later:
- **Conductor ✅** — it *is* a dependency graph: processes with steps, some parallel, some sequential, an owner defining the shape and doers receiving reminders. Nested structure, linked records, instantiable templates, roll-up views aren't decoration there — **they are the shape of the problem.** The spec literally describes a spine with limbs; Notion is built for spine-with-limbs.
- **Antiphon ❌** — a Motif has **no children, no dependencies, no parent, no properties to filter on.** Its entire structure is *"it's number 77."* Every Notion affordance — nesting, tagging, databases, views — is **an invitation to organise, and Antiphon exists precisely because organising was the tax that broke the writing habit.**
- **The one-line test: Conductor's value is in the relationships between things; Antiphon's value is in there being no relationships at all.** Same toolkit, opposite verdicts. (Consistent with the boundary tests — different headspaces: Conductor is owner-over-others, multi-person, structural; Antiphon is one voice, one line, no audience.)

### ▶ BEHAVIOUR — resolved 24 Jul (these were the gaps an agent would otherwise invent)

- **First launch is NOT empty.** Alphonse has **4 Motifs already restructured from Emberling**, going in immediately — so no crickets on day one. *(Still worth a decent empty state for the theoretical zero case, but it isn't the first impression.)*
- **▶ LOGIN LANDS ON A BLANK INPUT, NOT A DASHBOARD.** Decided 24 Jul. Landing on a dashboard means the first thing he meets is *the record of what he's done* — the scoreboard, i.e. the exact thing that made writing feel like performance. **Landing in the page means the app's opinion is "of course you're writing."** The catalogue is ONE DELIBERATE TAP away, for when he *chooses* to look back. *(Consistent with the whole write-first direction.)*
- **▶ AUTOSAVE — but only once the FIRST WORD arrives.** Non-negotiable; Alphonse insisted on autosave in Emberling and it "saves my life." ⚠️ **The delay-until-first-word matters:** it means an opened-but-unwritten Motif never persists, so **the numbering never fills with empty ghosts.** No. 78 exists only if something was written in it.
- **▶ EDITING OLD MOTIFS: YES — that's the point.** A Motif can be returned to and developed, so **depth is retroactive: the flame GROWS as the entry grows** ("glow up"). This is what makes a small flame an *invitation* rather than a verdict, and it's what turns the archive into a workshop.
- **▶ EFFORT: set at save, EDITABLE afterwards.** If a Motif can be developed further, it can be re-rated — Beep → Keep → Deep as the thinking deepens.
- **▶ WORD COUNT: visible only WHILE EDITING, never on first sight.** Present when he's working, absent when he's arriving. *(This is the day-5 "quiet the tracking" principle, applied precisely: the count informs, it doesn't greet.)*
- **▶ SEARCH: NEEDED, and it's the ONLY retrieval path.** With no tags, no chapters, and optional titles, **search is how Motif No. 200 gets found again.** Not necessarily day one, but it can't be forgotten — without it the archive becomes write-only.
- **❌ NO DECAY / NO AUTO-DELETE — considered and DEFUNCT (24 Jul).** A "glow up or gone with the wind" mechanic was floated (unimproved Beeps eventually deleted) and **rejected in Alphonse's words: *"all thoughts are still thoughts and still worth it when looking back."***
  - **Why this is right:** a Beep isn't a failure, it's a real moment where he had little to say — in a year that's evidence of a quiet week, and worth having. **Deleting them makes the archive a highlight reel rather than a record**, losing exactly what he values in the Record Book: honesty about what actually happened.
  - **It also keeps the framing coherent:** you cannot say *"a small flame is an invitation, not a punishment"* AND *"unimproved flames get deleted"* in the same app. Deletion is a punishment. One had to go, and it was the right one.
  - ⚠️ Note this also settles the parked **"Rake the coals"** decay idea (§3.4) for Antiphon 3.0: **resurfacing is fine, deletion is not.**

### ▶ SEARCH — plain in v0.1, semantic later (decided 24 Jul)
⚠️ **Search is the ONLY retrieval path** (no tags, no chapters, optional titles), so it can't be skipped — without it the archive is write-only.

- **v0.1 = plain keyword search.** Enough for the first months.
- **▶ SEMANTIC / AI SEARCH — YES, but LATER.** The case is real: what he'll actually search for is fuzzy — *"the one where I worked out the date was the problem"* — and he won't recall whether he wrote "date," "calendar," or "chronological." Keyword search fails exactly there, and a notebook of thoughts is the ideal case for semantic retrieval. **Three reasons it waits:**
  1. **It needs volume.** At 4 Motifs you eyeball the list; at 20 you scroll. Semantic search earns its keep somewhere past 50–100 entries — months away. Building it now buys infrastructure that can't be used yet.
  2. **It's more build than it sounds:** embeddings per Motif, vector storage, **re-embedding on every edit** (and Motifs *are* edited — that's the glow-up), plus per-query API cost. Not a small fire at 5cr/day.
  3. **It would be the first AI in an app deliberately stripped back.** Benign (retrieval, not generation — it never touches the page), but worth entering *deliberately* rather than by default.
- **🔒 THE BOUNDARY, INHERITED FROM EMBERLING AND NON-NEGOTIABLE: AI may help him FIND what he wrote; it NEVER helps him write it.** Emberling's rule was that the AI never writes journal text — **that rule survives into Antiphon.**
- **Design note for when it lands:** results surface the **Motif number and opening line**, never an AI summary. *He should be led back to his own words, not shown a paraphrase of them.*

## 3.2b — ▶ ANTIPHON LOGIN FRICTION (the daily "cha-cha") + the auth-migration question

**Raised 23 Jul — and the strongest argument turned out to be the LAST one Alphonse gave: "it nags me doing the 4-step cha-cha daily."** That's lived friction on the writing path, not a theoretical concern — and by Antiphon's own doctrine ("a feature waits until a real moment in the writing life needed it"), daily friction between him and the page IS that moment. Same family as day 5: *quiet the app, remove what stands between him and the page.*

**The actual ritual, as described (5 steps):** open the page → tap the input box → tap the saved password → Face ID → tap Login.

**⚠️ KEY INSIGHT — most of it is NOT fixable by changing auth systems:**
- **Steps 2–4 (tap box → tap password → Face ID)** are the **iOS per-origin credential behaviour already investigated and CLOSED (§3.2)**: the credential lives on the `phon.se` parent origin, so it auto-presents on Symphon and goes tap-first on the `al.phon.se` sibling. **~13cr already proved this is iOS policy, not code.** Moving to account-based auth would NOT reliably fix it — it's about ORIGIN, not form type. **Do not re-open §3.2, and do not justify the auth migration on this.**
- **Step 5 (tap Login)** IS fixable and is pure friction → **auto-submit on successful credential fill.** Small change.

**▶ ⚠️ CORRECTION (23 Jul, important): the daily-ness is DELIBERATE, not a defect.** Alphonse **uses incognito to journal on purpose** — so that if someone picks up his phone, there's no live session waiting for them to pry into. Incognito discards the session on close; that IS the protection. **So "persist the session" (Rép's earlier suggestion) would have quietly destroyed the security model he built for himself — do NOT do it.** The daily unlock is the price of a choice he'd make again.

**▶ ✅ TESTED 23 Jul — NULL RESULT. SAME BEHAVIOUR IN NORMAL *AND* INCOGNITO.** Alphonse changed the Antiphon passkey (now its own password-manager entry) and observed both modes: **no difference — still tap-first either way.** This kills BOTH live theories at once:
- The original §3.2 mechanism (one shared credential under the parent `phon.se`, Antiphon as a "lazy sibling") — **obsolete**, he now has his own entry and it changed nothing.
- The incognito theory — **ruled out**, a normal tab behaves identically.
- **⛔ SO: §3.2 STAYS CLOSED — PERMANENTLY.** Its *conclusion* (not fixable from the page) holds even though its stated *mechanism* was wrong. Three theories have now died across ~13cr. A fourth theory is not a plan. **Do not reopen, do not investigate, do not fire on this.** The daily unlock is simply the cost of iOS being iOS, plus a security posture Alphonse chose.

**▶ CODE READ 23 Jul — BOTH remaining "fixes" ALREADY EXIST in `src/routes/unlock.tsx`. Verified by reading the file:**
- **`autoFocus` IS present** on the password input (it was restored after the §3.2 debugging stripped it). **But iOS Safari deliberately ignores programmatic autofocus on page load** — an Apple platform policy so pages can't hijack the keyboard/viewport on arrival. So step 2 ("tap the input box") is **the same wall as the credential issue: correct code, overruled by iOS. NOT fixable from the page.**
- **Auto-submit IS already built** — `handlePasswordChange` detects a credential fill (empty → >1 char in a single change) and calls `formRef.current.requestSubmit()` after a 200ms timer, guarded by `autoSubmittedRef`. **So step 5 ("tap Login") should already be automatic when the password manager fills the field.**
- **▶ 🐛 CONFIRMED BUG (23 Jul): auto-submit does NOT fire after autofill.** Alphonse tested — after Face ID fills the field, the form does not log in; he still has to tap the button. So step 5 is real, and it IS a genuine bug (not iOS policy).
  - **Likely cause #1 (most probable):** password managers fill the input via the browser's **native value setter, which does not trigger React's `onChange`**. So `handlePasswordChange` never runs → the 200ms timer never starts → `requestSubmit()` never fires. React state still believes the field is empty (which would also explain any button-state oddity).
  - **Likely cause #2:** even if `onChange` fires, the guard `previousValue === "" && value.length > 1` demands the fill arrive as ONE change from a genuinely empty field. Character-by-character filling, or any prior stray change, blocks it.
  - **FIX (small, safe, self-contained):** don't rely on React's `onChange` alone. Attach a native `input`/`change` listener to the password element (or briefly poll `inputRef.current.value` on mount) to detect an autofilled value React doesn't know about; sync it into state and then `requestSubmit()`. Loosen the guard so it tolerates multi-step fills.
  - **Worth doing:** it's the ONLY genuinely fixable step in the whole login ritual (5 → 4 steps), it's isolated to `unlock.tsx`, and it touches the *door*, not the writing surface. Good candidate to ride along with the day-5 Antiphon fire. ⚠️ Antiphon is otherwise FROZEN — don't let this fire expand into anything else.
- ⛔ **Nothing else here is buildable.** Steps 2–4 are iOS policy across three dead theories and ~13cr. Do not reopen.

**▶ How this reframes the auth-migration question (interesting, but still ripe-later):** his threat model is *"someone picks up my unlocked phone and pries."* Against that, a shared passphrase in incognito is genuinely reasonable — but it costs him a persistent session anywhere, so the friction is permanent BY DESIGN. The version that would truly serve him is **real per-user auth with a biometric-gated session** — stay logged in, but require Face ID to open the app, so a person holding the phone still can't get in. ⚠️ **Feasibility caveat:** Antiphon is a browser-based PWA, and web apps have limited/no access to a native biometric app-lock — this may simply not be buildable on the current platform. Investigate feasibility BEFORE ever scoping it, and don't spend credits chasing it blind (see §3.2's ~13cr lesson).

**▶ THE AUTH MIGRATION (shared passphrase → real per-user auth) — still RIPE-LATER, judged on its own merits:**
- **Security (the real case):** `APP_PASSPHRASE` is one shared secret — can't be revoked per-device, can't be rotated without re-typing everywhere, write-only so it can't be read back. Meanwhile **Symphon already has proper per-user auth** (Supabase `authenticated`, `auth.uid() = user_id`, RLS on `herald_entries`/`suites`/`movements`). The *work* app has a stronger lock than the *private journal* — backwards.
- **Consistency:** two auth models = two things to maintain; one Supabase pattern would simplify.
- **NOT justified by the daily friction** (see above — that's fixed by session persistence instead).
- ⚠️ **THE RISK, unchanged:** existing entries have no `user_id`. Migration means touching **the one irreplaceable dataset in the whole suite** — Symphon data is regenerable, journal entries are not. Follow the Score precedent: spec it first, and seriously consider whether a simpler path avoids migration entirely. **Verify a read path works BEFORE removing the passphrase** — being locked out of his own archive is the nightmare case.
- Keep the writing surface untouched — auth changes the *door*, never the room.
- **Not a numbered fire.** Stays ripe-later until it earns a slot on security grounds alone.

## 5.0 — What it is
**Aspect°** — a public register of short reflective essays. Tagline: **"friction, observed."** Each piece is ONE thing noticed (usually a friction spotted while building), developed to ~500 words, essayistic and reflective in voice. Wordmark is *Aspect* + a degree mark (°) — the mark means the observer's *angle of view*, and a reading *taken*. Name chosen for meaning: **aspect = perspective**, which is how Alphonse works (he spots friction others walk past). Sense of the word: **observational** — the observer's stance, not the thing seen. *(Note: "friction, observed" is literally the name of a file in his `personal/` handover folder — the stance predates the name; Aspect just names what he was already doing.)*

## 5.1 — Identity (LOCKED)
- **Name:** Aspect° · **Tagline:** friction, observed (the first resolves with the domain; the tagline describes the practice — decided over "Friction · Observed" as the masthead).
- **Mark:** the degree sign, small, in brass. Appears in the wordmark, marks the threshold line of each essay, and renders section breaks. Earns its place three ways: unit of *angle*, a *reading taken*, and typographically quiet (rewards attention — the site's whole subject).
- **Design signature:** the **threshold** — each essay's first sentence set large in italic display type, marked with a °, that you "cross into"; body then settles to a calm reading measure.
- **Type:** Fraunces (display/titles), Newsreader (body, ~19px, ~64ch measure — sized for real reading), Spline Sans Mono (labels/meta). **Palette:** cool slate paper (#EAE8E1, deliberately NOT the AI-default cream), graphite ink, single brass accent (#8A6A2F). Dodges all three AI-design defaults.
- **Structure:** index-first (numbered register + one-line "draw" per piece) → each essay on its own page at `aspect.sg/<slug>`, own URL (shareable). Reading room, not a feed.

## 5.2 — The pipeline & ethic (LOCKED)
- **Two registers, two authors, kept separate:** the Record Book is Rép's account of the build; **Aspect is Alphonse's own writing.** Motifs originate as his private reflections (in Antiphon, his notebook), and only the good ones get **copied across and SANITISED** into public essays. *(Rép must NOT conflate the two — Aspect essays are his, hand-written.)*

The public never sees the raw private entry. **The reader never needs to know about the process** (decided 25 Jul — an earlier mock exposed obs./pub. dates and "filed" redaction marks; rejected as machinery the reader shouldn't see).
- **The right-friction thesis:** the whole design keeps friction where it belongs — sanitise-on-copy, deliberate-hand publishing, name-it-to-publish. "A thinking gate that happens to publish." This is Alphonse's own weight-matches-consequence motif applied to his tooling.
